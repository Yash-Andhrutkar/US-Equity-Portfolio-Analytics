01 — Performance

Cumulative performance against the benchmark

Cumulative return is rebased to the start of the selected range. Drawdown is always measured against the running peak of the full sample, so a decline that began before the range is not concealed by it.

02 — Allocation & risk

Capital weight is not risk

Capital weight is how much of the portfolio a holding occupies. Risk contribution is how much of the portfolio's volatility it is responsible for, accounting for its own volatility and its correlation with everything else. The two are rarely equal.

04 — Technical signals

MA50 / MA200 crossovers

Historical trend description only. A crossover records what price did, not what it will do. Moving averages are computed on the full price history, because a 200-day average requires 200 prior observations to exist at all.

05 — Strategy backtest

Buy & hold against the moving-average rule

Positions are shifted one trading day before being applied to returns, so no future information enters the result. No transaction costs, slippage or taxes are modelled. The rule reduces drawdown on some holdings and gives up return on others — it is not superior in general.

06 — Monte Carlo

10,000 simulated one-year scenarios

Daily portfolio returns are drawn from a normal distribution parameterized by the portfolio's historical daily mean and volatility, then compounded over 252 trading days. Real markets exhibit fat tails, volatility clustering and regime changes that this does not capture, so extreme outcomes are under-represented.
